## 1. Purpose and boundary

This document defines a finite retailer portfolio and relationship strategy supporting the [RAM launch catalog and minimum useful coverage definition](./RAM-LAUNCH-CATALOG-AND-COVERAGE.md). It is operator planning, not a retailer registry, rights profile, CRM, acquisition plan, or public-eligibility policy.

Track C selects strategically valuable relationships and pursues commercial/data cooperation. It does not own Atlas retailer identity, Mercury observation or rights authority, publication eligibility, Cheapest/Pick ordering, or Compass recommendation authority.

## 2. Portfolio doctrine

Hardware Radar should build a deliberately mixed launch portfolio combining:

- major recognizable hardware/electronics retailers;
- specialist PC/component retailers;
- smaller retailers where a direct relationship can provide disproportionate strategic value; and
- retailers with meaningful overlap against the launch RAM slots.

Retailer value to Hardware Radar and retailer value to Mercury are related but distinct and must not collapse into one score or authority.

| Hardware Radar consumer value | Mercury/data value |
|---|---|
| Consumer recognition and trust | Structured feeds or APIs |
| Useful RAM selection | Stable retailer/product identifiers |
| Competitive pricing and comparison value | Explicit condition and shipping evidence |
| Catalog overlap and listing stability | Offer/bundle/conditional-price classification |
| US geographic relevance | Acquisition reliability and replay support |
| Shopper usefulness | Explicit acquisition, display, comparison, and retention rights |
| Sustainable commercial potential | Data-quality cooperation |

Commercial status remains independent from market authority. Keep these capabilities separate:

```text
OBSERVED
PUBLICLY COMPARABLE
RECOMMENDABLE
AFFILIATE ENABLED
```

Affiliate approval, commission rate, payout terms, tracking quality, or relationship strength grants none of observation, retention, comparison, publication, Cheapest, Pick, or recommendation authority. A retailer need not be an affiliate partner to participate in governed comparison when Mercury and publication policy independently permit it. Affiliate economics never affect governed comparison ordering.

This portfolio follows the [Commercial-First, Capability-Separated Partnership Doctrine](./HARDWARE-RADAR-PRODUCT-EVOLUTION.md#commercial-first-capability-separated-partnership-doctrine). Hardware Radar may pursue an independently useful commercial relationship before ideal data capabilities exist. Approval provides access to inspect the program's actual tools and terms; feed/API, comparison, display, retention, historical-analysis, and other Mercury capabilities are classified separately afterward. Lack of those capabilities is not itself a reason to reject an otherwise valuable relationship, while incompatible program terms may still be disqualifying.

## 3. Retailer silence and complementary paths

Hardware Radar previously contacted Newegg and Best Buy seeking data-use/rights clarification and received no response. Silence is neither permission nor denial. It contributed to pursuing governed independent evidence through DataForSEO because lawful acquisition could not remain dependent on retailer replies.

Three complementary paths should proceed in parallel:

1. **Governed independent acquisition** — for example DataForSEO under its own rights and Mercury controls.
2. **Direct retailer relationships** — affiliate programs, authorized feeds/APIs, explicit rights, deep links, attribution, and data-quality cooperation.
3. **Transitional curated evidence** — explicitly approved operator-curated evidence for narrow launch paths under existing Mercury governance.

The curated bridge remains bootstrap infrastructure and retires progressively when automated Mercury acquisition reaches governed parity. Curated history must never be rewritten as automated evidence. Direct retailer cooperation is desirable but cannot become a universal acquisition dependency.

## 6. Relationship-state model

Track relationship state independently across four dimensions:

1. `COMMERCIAL_AFFILIATE`
2. `PRODUCT_FEED_API`
3. `DATA_RIGHTS`
4. `DATA_QUALITY_BUSINESS_RELATIONSHIP`

Each dimension may use:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `NOT_CONTACTED` | No operator-confirmed outreach. |
| `CONTACTED` | Outreach sent; no substantive outcome implied. |
| `RESPONDED` | A response exists but no discovery or approval implied. |
| `DISCOVERY` | Requirements/terms are being explored. |
| `APPLIED` | A formal commercial or access application was submitted. |
| `APPROVED` | That exact dimension was explicitly approved. |
| `DECLINED` | That exact request was declined. |
| `DORMANT` | Work is intentionally inactive without erasing history. |
| `UNKNOWN` | Current state is not established. |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | The dimension does not apply. |

`APPROVED` is always dimension-specific. Affiliate approval plus feed approval can coexist with data rights still in `DISCOVERY`; there is no generic “approved retailer.” At current scale this belongs in reviewed documentation/operator notes, not a CRM or runtime workflow.
