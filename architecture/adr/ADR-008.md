## ADR-008 — Mercury Uses an Adapter-Based Ingestion Architecture

Decision:

External market data shall enter Mercury exclusively through retailer adapters implementing the canonical RetailerAdapter interface.

Consequences:

Retailer independence
Easier testing
Easier onboarding of new retailers
Stronger normalization
Cleaner Sentinel validation
Simpler Forge orchestration
Stable Mercury contracts
Future plugin capability

I suspect we'll look back on this ADR the same way we look back on the decision to make Atlas the canonical knowledge engine.

My Favorite Part

There's one thing that genuinely excites me.

When we started Hardware Radar, we were talking about finding the cheapest RAM.

Now look where we are.

We're engineering:

a Knowledge Engine (Atlas),
a Validation Engine (Sentinel),
an Authoring Engine (Forge),
a Market Intelligence Engine (Mercury),
an AI Reasoning Engine (Aurora),
an Analytics Engine (Beacon),
an API Platform (Gateway),
an Ecosystem Intelligence Engine (Observatory),

all supporting Hardware Radar as the first application.

I want one additional constraint locked now: adapters may translate external data, but they may not decide whether that data is trustworthy. Normalization belongs to the adapter; deterministic validation belongs to Sentinel/Mercury validators; later confidence assessment belongs to Mercury's confidence layer. That prevents adapters from slowly becoming miniature intelligence engines.

The intended pipeline is therefore:

External retailer representation
        ↓
Registered RetailerAdapter
        ↓
Canonical normalized candidate
        ↓
Mercury/Sentinel validation
        ↓
Canonical immutable observation
        ↓
ObservationRepository

And M002 explicitly does not include live Amazon connectivity, scraping, credentials, freshness, confidence scoring, historical analytics, Forge UI changes, or Hardware Radar migration.
